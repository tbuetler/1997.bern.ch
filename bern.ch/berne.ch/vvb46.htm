Excursions

Ideal situation: a look at the map of Switzerland shows Berne to be an ideal point of departure for excursions: Alps (30 min.), Bernese Oberland, Bernese Mittelland (Emmental [Emme Valley], Seeland [Lakeland]), Jura, Lake Geneva region, Central Switzerland, Valais. In short, practically the whole of Switzerland is easily accessible from Berne for either half-day or full-day excursions. Berne Excursions: Tourist Office in Railway Station.

City Tours

Round-tripsThese can be enjoyed on foot or by coach, and reserved at the tourist office in Berne. A multilingual guide will point out the sights, such as the Clock Tower, the Bear Pits, the Rosengarten from which visitors have a unique view over the mediaeval Old Town, the 11 historic fountains dating from around 1550, the 6 km/4 miles of arcades and shops, etc.
Clock TowerGuided tours for individual persons and groups. The Clock Tower was the western gate of the city from 1218 to 1256, when the city was first expanded. The astronomical clock and figure-play dating from 1530 are world-famous.

Half-day Excursions

GruyèresThis excursion takes you to the mediaeval village of Gruyères, perched on top of a hill - a picturesque gem in the middle of rural Fribourg. The castle, dating from the 13th century, is open to the public an tourists will also admire the late Gothic houses lining the village's main street. The modern working dairy, where the famous Gruyère cheese is made, is also worth a visit, and the cheese and other dairy products can be tasted.
Emmental TourA tour through the Emmental, via Moosegg, to Lueg, a vantage point offering a splendid panorama of the Alps and the rolling hills of the Emme Valley (Emmental). This is where the world-famous cheese, with its well-known holes, is made. A dairy can be visited where the cheese can be sampled.
Mount NiesenWith the Lötschberg railway from Berne to Mülenen, where the Niesen cable railway starts. Breathtaking ascent in two sections up to the 2'362 m/7'747 ft summit of the pyramid-shaped Mount Niesen, from where there are wonderful views of Lake Thun and Lake Brienz, the snowy peaks of the Bernese Oberland, the Swiss central plain and the Jura hills. (May to October)
Mount StockhornThe first part of this trip takes you along the Aare valley via Thun and Wimmis, and to Erlenbach in the Simmental (Simmen Valley). From here the cable-car ascends to the summit of the Stockhorn (2'190 m / 7'183 ft). Impressive views over Lake Thun, the Alps, the Bernese lowlands and the Jura.
Photo Safari through
Mediaeval Market Towns		This tour takes in the mediaeval market towns of Aarberg (built round its large market square), Erlach (11th century), Le Landeron, La Neuveville (with the famous "Rue du Marché"), Ligerz (a picturesque vineyard village), and Büren an der Aare. Visitors can also call in at a wine cellar on Lake Bienne if they wish. Travelling time: 2 ½ hours.

Day Excursions

Mount SchilthornThis tour takes visitors through Thun, Spiez and Interlaken to Lauterbrunnen, where the Trämmelbach Falls are well worth a visit, and on to Stechelberg. From here the longest cable-car in the Alps leads you up to the summit of the Schilthorn. Lunch in the "Piz Gloria" revolving restaurant, where a James Bond film was taken, with time to admire an all-round view of the Alps. On the return journey stop in Interlaken and then continuation along the northern shores of Lake Thun to Berne.
JungfraujochThis tour goes right into the Bernese Oberland. From Lauterbrunnen by rack railway to Wengen and via the Kleine Scheidegg up to the Jungfraujoch (3'454 m/ 11'329 ft). From here the view of the Aletsch Glacier is breathtaking. One can visit the Ice Palace and the Sphinx Terrace, etc. The return journey is made via Kleine Scheidegg and Grindelwald to Interlaken, which can also be visited, and home to Berne.
Zermatt - MatterhornThis route leads you through the Lötschberg rail tunnel, where vehicles are loaded on the train to Visp in the Rhone valley, the market garden of Switzerland. Then it takes the Matter valley road into the mountains to Täsch, where the road ends. The last part of the route up to Zermatt, where no vehicles are allowed, is by rail. Then visitors will have time to look around this popular resort at the foot of the 4'478 m/14'560 ft Matterhorn. Those who feel adventurous can take a trip on the rack railway from Zermatt to the Gornergrat (3'089 m/10'132 ft)
Lucerne - Mount Pilatus and Mount TitlisFrom Berne along the shores of Lake Brienz, past the Swiss Open Air Museum at Ballenberg, over the Brünig Pass to Alpnachstad. From there by rack railway to the summit of Pilatus (2'129 m/6'982 ft), where a splendid panorama of the surrounding mountains and Lake Lucerne opens up. The return journey starts with the descent from Pilatus by cable-cars to Kriens on the outskirts of Lucerne, with time to look around and visit the Swiss Transport Museum.
Within convenient distance is also well-kown Mount Titlis.
Lake GenevaThis is a tour into the French-speaking part of Switzerland. The route leads via the mediaeval market towns of Murten, Payerne and Moudon to the Swiss Riviera, as this area is often called. From Lausanne-Ouchy visitors take a boat to Montreux. Visit to the 12th century castle of Chillon. The return journey leads through the vineyards high above the lake along the famous "Corniche" road to Bulle and Fribourg.
Grindelwald/InterlakenThis mountain village is in the heart of the Bernese Alps, at the foot of an impressive group of mountains - the Wetterhorn, the Schreckhorn and the Eiger. Grindelwald is the starting point for trips to the First, the Kleine Scheidegg and the Jungfraujoch. The return journey includes a short stop in Interlaken, which is a well-known holiday resort where, during the summer, the Tell plays (Swiss national hero) or the Beatus Caves on the shores of Lake Thun can be visited.
Three Countries' TourThis tour goes northwards to Basel and then across into the French Alsace, taking the famous wine road along the foot of the Vosges hills. The route passes through typical Alsace villages to the mediaeval town of Colmar, which is famous for it's Isenheimer altar. After lunch the tour continues across the Rhine to Freiburg im Breisgau and through the Höllental to the Titi Lake in the Black Forrest.
Jura Tour with PicnicThis is a coach trip to the north-western part of Switzerland, the Jura, which is the home of the Swiss watchmaking industry. It is still somewhat off the beaten track of mass tourism. In the summer visitors can enjoy a ride in a horse-drawn carriage - in winter a horse-drawn sleigh - followed by a picnic in the open meadows or lunch in a typical small restaurant. During the drive through the Franches Montagnes, which is a horse-breeding area, visitors will be able to visit the Bellelay Abbey and the watch museum in La Chaux-de-Fonds. Travelling time: 5 hours (plus approximately 1 hour for horse-drawn carriage ride).

Evening Excursions

Candle-light Dinner in a CastleBy coach or train to a mediaeval castle near Berne for a candle-light dinner. On arrival visitors will be wellcomed by heralds and then served an aperitif in the gardens or courtyard. Dinner is eaten in the knights' hall to the accompaniment of traditional folk groups, pipers, drummers and singers. Later there is dancing to live music. (Only for groups, more than 20 people)
Evening Excursions by Boat (minimum 80 Participants)Among the most popular excursions from Berne is a boat trip on either nearby Lake Thun and Lake Brienz in the Bernese Oberland, or on Lake Bienne in the Bernese Mittelland. Dinner on board with music and traditional entertainment. In the case of very large groups, several boats can be roped together for the trip, and if desired it is possible to go ashore, e.g. to visit St. Peter's Island (home of Jean-Jacques Rousseau), or the Oberhofen castle.
